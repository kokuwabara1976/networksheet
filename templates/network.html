What does your network look like?

Your next task is to draw your network in the grid below. First, use the Selection Tool (the red arrow) to drag each contact to the appropriate square, based upon their status and workplace. Their status may be based on their formal rank or your own perception of how much more or less senior (older, more experienced, etc) they are.

Next, switch to the Connection Tool (the plus sign) and draw connections by clicking on one contact and dragging the cursor to another as if you are drawing a line (you may not see the line until you reach the other dot). Connect any two contacts if they are good friends or colleagues who talk to or spend time with each other on their own at least every few months. If you are not sure, assume they are not connected. To erase a tie, use the Selection Tool to highlight it and hit Delete (Mac: fn + delete) on your keyboard.

Here is a sample. Do not worry about being perfectly accurate. More important is that you take the time to think about what you know or do not know about your network.

Once your network is drawn, think about what it looks like. Do you see any empty cells? Crowded cells? Next to each person's initials are letters indicating which types of support or benefits the person provides: T=technical support (problem-solving, expertise), N=news and information (opportunities, events, or happenings at work), P=political support (securing resources, positions, opportunities), G=guidance (wisdom, feedback, mentoring), I=inspiration (energy, courage, creativity), F=friendship (social support, companionship). Do you see any missing benefits?
